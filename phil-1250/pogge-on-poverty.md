# Pogge on Poverty

### Pogge on Ending Poverty

Pogge's superficial response to the question of "Why do I feel like the world is getting so much better despite there being so many deaths due to poverty?" is income distribution. According to Pogge, income inequality is rapidly rising, and has risen, on both a global and national scale (except for Latin American countries). His reasoning for why this has occurred comes down to *regulatory capture*.

Individuals paying money to politicians to influence legislation and law is the act of regulatory capture (lobbying). Corporations and rich individuals will invest money into lobbying - and it is seen as an investment because they are hoping for an eventual return from their lobbying. Pogge uses the following statistic: "The income share of the poorest half of the population dropping by more than half from 26.4% to 12.8%. That of the top 1 percent rose 2.6-fold from 8.95% to 23.50%; that of the top 1/10 percent rose 4.6-fold from 2.65% to 12.28%; that of the top 1/100 percent rose 7-fold from 0.86% to 6.04% (Saez Table A3).

Seven examples of the headwind blowing in the face's of the poor:
1. Remaining protectionism that blocks imports. Subsidies in agriculture make it difficult for smaller countries to compete.
2. Negative externalities that aren't monetarily compensated.
3. Strong intellectual property rights for pharmeceutical companies.
4. Illicit financial flows towards western banking systems.
5. Lack of labor standards that force poor countries to offer up more exploitable conditions.
6. Poor countries are badly governed, and much of this was caused by illegitimate rulers selling their own countries natural resources.
7. The arms trade profits off selling weapons to poor countries that contribute to internal repression. 

The responsibility relies on the governments of the more powerful countries and in turn, the corporations and citizens that give rise to these powerful governments. 

### Pogge on Global Poverty

According to Pogge, each individual is entitled to a scheme of basic equal liberties, and these basic equal liberties must be secured. Moreover, social and economic inequalities should be arranged such that the people in the lowest socio-economic positions are as well-off as possible. (Inequality is only justified if they benefit the bottom). 

The problems of global poverty are the largest by sheer quantity (more so than political repression and torture). Individuals not only have a duty to help out the poor, but are committing a great crime by merely standing by and watching.
