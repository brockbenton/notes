# PHIL 1250 @ CU Boulder (Fall 2026)

## Course Description
This course explores poverty, power, and patriotism through the prism
of global social justice and human rights, paying particular attention to
the moral, legal, and political framework through which these issues
may be investigated. Among the questions we will especially focus on
are the relationship between sovereignty and power, the conditions
under which legitimate state sovereignty and authority over a people are
best established and, whether “perpetual peace” through diplomacy is
possible in the absence of a world state.

We will also inquire into whether democratic forms of government are
the best forms of government and whether non-democratic governments
might offer alternative, more defensible forms of statecraft. What might
alternative models of statecraft and sovereign power look like?

We will explore, moreover, the causes of and reasons for global poverty,
and consider arguments for why wealthy countries have a moral duty
and obligation to assist others in severe poverty and distress.
We will consider the fraught relationship between the economic system
of capitalism and poverty, probing the extent to which capitalism is
implicated in economic and social inequality.

We will also engage the often-misunderstood idea of patriotism: what
does the word mean, exactly? How has it been understood historically?
How is it understood today? How has it been manipulated for political
ends by those who hope to gain and maintain power? These questions
inevitably lead to the discussion of nationalism, over against
cosmopolitanism: do our duties extend to our fellow countrymen and
women in virtue of our shared identity as American citizens, or do our
duties extend beyond our physical borders to the broader global
community?
