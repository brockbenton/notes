# Peter Singer

## The why and how of effective altruism

*Altruism.* Concern for the well-being and life of others independently of personal benefit. 

Singer presents a though experiment: Say a little girl is ran over by a van in a street. If you are walking by, would you help? Most people would say yes - but Singer asks the following question: Does it really matter that you don't have to walk around these children as you walk down your street? Millions of children die per year, from known-to-be preventable diseases. Can we reduce that death toll?

Much of us spend money on things we don't *need*. Instead, we could give up our money to various charities that are aimed at reducing the exact death toll discussed in the thought experiment.

Billionaires like Bill Gates are able to give a large share of their income and *actually* make an impact - but what if you don't have that type of money? Can you even make an impact with so little money? Singer says there are 4 questions to be answered:

1. *How much of a difference can I make?* A small amount each year adds up. 
2. *Am I expected to abandon my career?* Choose a high-impact career in which you are making a lot of money, so you can give a lot of money. 
3. *Isn't charity bureaucratic and ineffective?* Providing a guide dog for a blind American costs the same as the cost in curing upwards of 2,000 individuals in developing countries. You can research about which organizations are effective are which aren't. 
4. *Isn't it a burden to give up so much?* Many people enjoy giving and receive lots of fulfillment. 

*My Opposition: Everything is self-serving in the technical sense. But even with that, something can be self-serving, and also societally-serving.* 

## What is utilitarianism?

Utilitarianism is an ethical theory that falls under the overarching ethics of consequentualism. The aim of utilitarianism is to maximize utility, or the happiness and well-being of all affected individuals. As such, for utilitarianism, an act is morally good if it produces the greatest happiness or pleasure to the greatest number of people. 

* *Act Utilitarianism.* Judges each action in isolation to determine if it produces the greatest net happiness. 
* *Rule Utilitarianism.* Judges actions based on adherence to moral rules whose general acceptance would produce the best overall consequences for society.
