# Kant's Moral Philosophy

**Kant's Context.** Hume's perspective on morals was consequential: what is moral is what is agreeable to ourselves and others - our moral frameworks are built from taste and consequence, not some sort of principle inherent to humankind. Hume suggested that morality was more a subset of anthropology - not something divine or religious like Kant would like to believe.

## Noumenal vs. Phenomenal

The cosmos is split between morals (noumenal) and everyday life (phenomenal). 
* The noumenal world is the realm of morality. Kant's attempt is to formulate a sort of science like Newtonian mechanics that explains the noumenal world - to make morality intellectually rigorous and serious. If the universe is merely Newtonian, there is no sense of freedom since the world must then be purely determinant. Ethics is, then, reletavized and subjectivized.
* The phenomenal world is derived from sense and perception, driven by the laws of causality and Newtonian mechanics.
